# Curated abstract-mark visual language

Read this reference only for abstract personal marks when the user asks to use their curated logo taste. It distills visual preferences from supplied examples; it does not license copying those marks.

## Shared preferences

- Abstract identity before literal metaphor, initials, or software symbols.
- Bold topology recognizable from silhouette and internal voids.
- Modular systems: repeated blocks, capsules, shards, ribbons, or line segments governed by one geometric rule.
- Central or off-center negative space used as an active shape.
- Controlled anomaly: one missing, shifted, split, or differently colored component prevents mechanical blandness.
- Mix of hard geometry and generous rounding rather than uniformly sharp or uniformly soft forms.
- Rotational, interlocking, nested, or distributed compositions; perfect symmetry may be broken deliberately.
- Flat construction, no gradients or illustrative texture. One dominant color; optional second color with a structural role.
- Enough internal rhythm to feel designed, while retaining a simplified micro variant for 16–32 px.

## Useful compositional families

Use these as ingredients, not templates:

- distributed modules organized by invisible alignment
- interwoven bands around a non-circular void
- broken rings made from irregular tiles rather than a loading arc
- nested frames with displaced edges
- solid organic masses separated by precise cuts
- parallel strokes whose varying lengths imply a larger form
- asymmetric capsule grids
- opposing quadrants that generate a distinctive central void
- radial organic modules with unequal spacing or scale
- triadic folded shapes with figure/ground ambiguity

Create new topology by combining principles from at least two families. Change element count, spatial logic, void shape, and balance—not only color or corner radius.

## Avoid

- direct reconstruction of any supplied reference
- generic loaders, suns, flowers, network-node diagrams, or crypto-style emblems
- forcing initials or letters unless the user selects a typographic direction
- decorative complexity that disappears without leaving a strong base silhouette
- several interchangeable variants differing only in rotation, stroke, or palette

Judge candidates first in monochrome at 16, 32, and 128 px. Add a second color only after topology works.
